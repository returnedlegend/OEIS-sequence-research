# IDF and non-atomic families using maximal prime-power factors of the denominator

Let a,b be coprime positive integers, a>=2, and write

  b=product_{p in P} p^{e_p},   |P|>=2.

Assume

  a < p^{e_p} for every p in P.                         (1)

Put t=a/b, S=N_0[t], and T=N_0[t,t^{-1}]. Then:

1. The multiplicative monoid S\{0} is IDF and non-atomic, for arbitrary a
   satisfying (1).
2. If a is a prime power, then T\{0} is also IDF and non-atomic.

The unit groups are {1} and t^Z, respectively. No novelty claim is made.
Condition (1) compares a with each maximal prime-power factor of b; the
individual primes dividing b may be smaller than a.

Examples covered by the stronger condition (1) include the cyclic semiring N_0[5/144], since
the maximal prime-power factors of 144 are 16 and 9, and the reciprocal
semiring N_0[4/225,225/4], since those of 225 are 9 and 25.
For t=5/144, an explicit element with no atomic factorization is

  243/128 = 1 + 4*t + 630*t^2.

It is b-smooth and lies strictly between 1 and 2, so section 8 applies.
It actually has no atom divisor in either the cyclic semiring or
N_0[5/144,144/5]; see RECIPROCAL_ALGORITHM.md for the latter statement.

## 1. Real conductor

Because at least two distinct maximal prime-power factors exceed a, b>a^2.
Thus 0<t<1 and

  C=(a-1)b/(b-a)=(a-1)/(1-t)<a.

Put R=Z[1/b]. Every x in R with x>=C belongs to S. To prove this, write
x=N/b^n and induct on n. The n=0 case is immediate. Choose c in
{0,...,a-1} so N-c*b^n is divisible by a, using gcd(a,b)=1. Then

  y=(x-c)/t=(N-c*b^n)/(a*b^{n-1})

has denominator dividing b^{n-1}, and

  y >= (C-(a-1))/t = C.

The induction hypothesis gives y in S, hence x=c+t*y in S.

## 2. Near-one smooth elements

Choose two distinct primes p,q in P. For each positive integer L, let
E_L=phi(a^L). The multiplicative group generated by p^{E_L} and q^{E_L}
is dense in the positive real numbers: log(p)/log(q) is irrational, so
the additive subgroup generated by their logarithms is dense in R.
Every member h of this group is b-smooth and satisfies h-1 in a^L R,
including when negative powers occur.

Choose h_L from this group with

  1+C*t^L < h_L < 1+(C+1)*t^L.

Then z_L=(h_L-1)/t^L belongs R and exceeds C. Section 1 gives z_L in S,
so h_L=1+t^L*z_L belongs S. The h_L tend to 1 from above. They have an
infinite pairwise distinct subsequence; use that subsequence below.

## 3. Units

Choose any prime ell dividing a; it does not divide b. The only
nonnegative Laurent-polynomial expression for 1 at t is the constant 1.
Indeed a negative exponent contributes at least t^{-1}>1. In the absence
of negative exponents, a positive constant term forces that term to be 1
and all others to vanish. If the constant term is zero, every term has
positive ell-adic valuation, so their sum cannot be 1.

If two nonnegative Laurent polynomials evaluate to mutually inverse
elements, their formal product therefore equals the single constant
monomial 1. Both factors must be monomials of coefficient 1. Consequently
T has unit group t^Z. The same argument with ordinary polynomials gives
the unit group {1} for S.

## 4. Large coefficients force reducibility in either monoid

Suppose beta=f(t)+a*t^j with f in N_0[X]. For the elements h_L of section 2
with L>=j, put

  w_L=(beta/h_L-f(t))/t^j.

Since h_L is a unit of R and h_L-1 belongs a^L R,

  beta/h_L-f(t)=a*t^j+beta*(h_L^{-1}-1)

shows w_L belongs R. Moreover w_L tends to a>C. For sufficiently large L,
the conductor gives w_L in S and hence beta/h_L=f(t)+t^j*w_L in S.

Thus beta=h_L*(beta/h_L) is a factorization in both S and T. Both factors
are nonunits for all sufficiently large indices of our distinct subsequence.
For h_L this follows from h_L>1, h_L tending to 1, and discreteness of the
relevant unit group. The second factor tends to beta; a compact positive
interval contains finitely many units, and each such unit can equal
beta/h_L for at most one of the distinct h_L.

Therefore any element admitting a polynomial expression with a coefficient
at least a is reducible in both multiplicative monoids.

Every S-atom other than the possible atom t consequently has an expression

  beta=sum_{i=0}^n c_i*t^i,  0<=c_i<a,  c_0,c_n>0.       (2)

Indeed an expression with zero constant coefficient factors out the
nonunit t, and atomicity would force the remaining factor to be 1.
For every T-atom, multiplying an arbitrary Laurent expression by a power
of the unit t produces an associate with least exponent 0, and this
associate has the form (2).

## 5. The numerator valuations are uniformly bounded at denominator primes

For (2), write beta=N/b^n, where

  N=sum_{i=0}^n c_i*a^i*b^{n-i} >= b^n.

This fraction need not be reduced. For p in P, the leading term
c_n*a^n has valuation v_p(c_n)<e_p, because

  0<c_n<a<p^{e_p}.

Every other term is divisible by b and has valuation at least e_p.
Consequently

  v_p(N)=v_p(c_n)<e_p.                                  (3)

The conclusion also holds when n=0. Thus the part of N supported on P
divides the fixed integer

  D=product_{p in P} p^{e_p-1}.

## 6. IDF in the cyclic case

Fix a nonzero x in S, and let X be the part of its reduced numerator
supported outside P. If an atom beta of form (2) divides x in S, the
cofactor belongs R. Hence the outside-P part M of N divides X. By (3),

  N=d*M,   d|D,   M|X.

There are only finitely many possibilities for N. Since N>=b^n, there
are only finitely many possible n, hence finitely many values N/b^n.
The possible exceptional atom t adds at most one value. Units are trivial,
so this proves IDF for S\{0}.

## 7. IDF in the reciprocal case when a is a prime power

Suppose now a=ell^s. For a normalized atom (2), the constant term in N has
valuation v_ell(c_0)<s, and all remaining terms have valuation at least s.
Therefore

  v_ell(N)=v_ell(c_0)<s.                                 (4)

Fix x in T, and let X be the part of its reduced numerator supported
outside P union {ell}. Every denominator in T is supported on this union.
If a normalized atom beta divides x in T, its numerator consequently has
the form

  N=ell^j*d*M,   0<=j<s,   d|D,   M|X.

There are finitely many such N, and N>=b^n again gives finitely many
possible normalized atom values. Every atom-divisor class of x has a
normalized representative, which remains a divisor of x because the
normalizing power of t is a unit. This proves IDF for T\{0}.

## 8. Direct failure of atomicity in the cyclic case

Let beta be a b-smooth atom of S. It is not t, which has numerator a
coprime to b. Its numerator N from (2) is b-smooth, so (3) gives

  N<=D=b/product_{p in P} p<b.

Because N>=b^n, this forces n=0. Thus beta is an integer, and atomicity
forces it to be an ordinary prime, necessarily a prime below a dividing b.
In particular every b-smooth atom is at least 2.

Choose h_L with 1<h_L<2. It is a b-smooth nonunit of S. Every atom in a
factorization of h_L would be b-smooth: outside-P valuations of all
S-elements are nonnegative and must sum to zero. Thus every such atom
would be an integer at least 2, making their product at least 2. This is
impossible. Consequently h_L has no factorization into atoms, proving
non-atomicity of S\{0}. More strongly it has no atom divisor: both an
atom divisor and its cofactor would be b-smooth and at least1, while the
atom would be an integer at least2.

## 9. Direct failure of atomicity in the reciprocal prime-power case

Suppose a=ell^s. Define the positive real-valued function

  I(x)=x*t^{-v_ell(x)/s},  x in T\{0}.

It is multiplicative and invariant under multiplication by units t^k.
Furthermore I(x)>=1 for all x, with equality exactly at the units. To see
this, express x as a sum of Laurent monomials, repeating monomials to
absorb integer coefficients. If m is the smallest exponent occurring,
then v_ell(x)>=s*m, while x>=t^m>=t^{v_ell(x)/s}. Equality throughout
requires the expression to contain just one monomial of coefficient 1,
which is a unit. Any expression with at least two terms gives strict
inequality in its first real-number comparison.

Consider an atom supported on the primes in P union {ell}, and normalize
it to (2) by a unit. Its numerator has no primes outside that union, so
(3) and (4) give

  N<=ell^{s-1}*D < a*b < b^2.

Since N>=b^n, its degree n is at most 1. If n=0, the normalized atom is
an integer c_0>=2 and its ell-adic valuation is nonnegative, so
I(beta)>=beta>=2>1+t. If n=1, its two positive coefficients give
beta>=1+t; again v_ell(beta)=v_ell(c_0)>=0 and therefore

  I(beta)>=beta>=1+t.

Thus every atom supported on P union {ell} has I-value at least 1+t.

Choose h_L with 1<h_L<1+t. It is b-smooth and nonunit, with
v_ell(h_L)=0, so I(h_L)=h_L<1+t. In any atomic factorization of h_L,
every atom must be supported on P union {ell}, because valuations outside
that union are nonnegative on T and sum to zero. Multiplicativity of I
would then give I(h_L)>=1+t, a contradiction. Hence h_L cannot factor
into atoms, proving non-atomicity of T\{0}. More strongly such h_L has no atom divisor:
any atom divisor would be supported on P union {ell} and have I-value
at least1+t, whereas its cofactor has I-value at least1.

## 10. Infinite actual divisors and divisor classes

The near-one elements also give a useful stronger contrast to IDF. Choose
an integer B>2C. For sufficiently large L, 1<h_L<2. Because h_L is
b-smooth, B/h_L belongs R, and B/h_L>B/2>C. The conductor puts B/h_L
in S. Thus infinitely many distinct h_L divide the same element B in
both S and T.

Their classes are distinct in S because its units are trivial. They are
also pairwise nonassociate in T, even for arbitrary a: choose ell|a.
Each h_L has ell-adic valuation zero, whereas a unit t^k has valuation
k*v_ell(a), so h_L/h_J can be a unit only if h_L=h_J.

An atomic IDF cancellative commutative monoid has finitely many divisor
classes for each element. Indeed its finitely many atom-divisor classes
give exponent vectors for factorizations, and these vectors form an
antichain in N_0^m. Dickson's lemma makes the factorization set finite;
every divisor factors into a subproduct of one of these factorizations.
This provides an alternative proof of non-atomicity in the two IDF cases
above.

## 11. Equivalent mixed pairs and precise scope

For positive coprime integers i,j,

  N_0[t^i,t^{-j}]=N_0[t,t^{-1}].

Bezout solutions to iu-jv=1 and iu-jv=-1 can be made nonnegative by
adding a large multiple of (j,i), proving both inclusions. Thus the
reciprocal result covers all such mixed pairs and their transposes.

For a with at least two distinct prime factors, the argument does not
prove reciprocal IDF: c_0<a need not make v_ell(c_0)<v_ell(a) at each
prime ell dividing a, so cancellation can invalidate the bound (4). Nevertheless section 10 still supplies infinitely many divisor
classes, so reciprocal atomicity and IDF cannot both hold in that case.
