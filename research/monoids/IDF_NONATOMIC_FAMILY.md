# A proved infinite family for part (b)

Let a,b be coprime positive integers such that 2 <= a < min{p : p divides b}, and suppose b has at least two distinct prime factors. Set t=a/b and S=N_0[t]. Then S\{0} is IDF and non-atomic. Consequently every pair (t,r) with r in N_0[t], and every swapped pair, belongs to part (b). For example (2/15,0), (2/15,1), and (2/15,4/225).

Put R=Z[1/b] and C=(a-1)b/(b-a). Because b has two distinct prime factors larger than a, b>a^2 and C<a.

## 1. Real conductor

Every x in R with x>=C lies in S. Write x=N/b^n. The a^n sums

A_e=sum_{i=0}^{n-1} e_i a^i b^{n-i}, 0<=e_i<a,

represent every residue modulo a^n exactly once: at the smallest index i where two digit strings differ, their difference has exact a-adic divisibility a^i, because b is coprime to a and the nonzero digit difference has magnitude less than a. Their maximum is

(a-1)b(b^n-a^n)/(b-a) < C b^n.

Thus N=A_e+k a^n for some k>=0, giving x=sum e_i t^i+k t^n in S. The n=0 case is immediate.

## 2. The only multiplicative unit is 1

Choose a prime ell dividing a. All elements of S have nonnegative ell-adic valuation. If u,u^{-1} lie in S, both have ell-adic valuation zero. Any polynomial expression for either must therefore have a positive constant coefficient, since every nonconstant term has positive ell-adic valuation. Hence u>=1 and u^{-1}>=1, forcing u=1.

## 3. Small smooth factors

For every sufficiently large integer L there exists a b-smooth positive rational u_L satisfying

1+C t^L < u_L < 1+(C+1)t^L,

and u_L-1 in a^L R. To see this, choose two different primes p,s dividing b, let T=phi(a^L), and use the subgroup generated by p^T and s^T. All its elements are congruent to 1 modulo a^L in R. It is dense in the positive reals, because log(p)/log(s) is irrational. Thus it meets the displayed interval.

Since z_L=(u_L-1)/t^L belongs to R and satisfies C<z_L<C+1, the conductor gives z_L in S. Therefore u_L=1+t^L z_L belongs to S, and u_L>1 is a nonunit. Also u_L tends to 1.

## 4. Large polynomial coefficients imply reducibility

Suppose a nonunit x in S has a polynomial representation containing a coefficient at least a, so x=f+a t^k for some f in S. For sufficiently large L>=k, set

y_L=(x/u_L-f)/t^k.

Because u_L is a unit of R and u_L-1 belongs to a^L R,

x/u_L-f = a t^k + x(u_L^{-1}-1)

belongs to a^k R. Hence y_L is in R. As L tends to infinity, y_L tends to a>C, so y_L>C for all sufficiently large L. The conductor yields y_L in S, and therefore x/u_L=f+t^k y_L belongs to S.

Choose L further so that u_L is not x. Then neither u_L nor x/u_L is 1, so x=u_L(x/u_L) is a factorization into two nonunits. Thus every atom admits only polynomial representations whose coefficients are less than a.

## 5. IDF

Let alpha be an atom other than possibly t. In any polynomial expression for alpha, its constant coefficient must be positive: otherwise alpha=t*h and atomicity forces h=1, giving alpha=t. Write alpha=sum_{i=0}^n c_i t^i with c_0>=1, c_n>=1 and 0<=c_i<a. Then

alpha=N/b^n, N=sum c_i a^i b^{n-i} >= b^n.

For each prime p dividing b, N is congruent to c_n a^n modulo p, and 1<=c_n<a<p. Thus gcd(N,b)=1; the displayed fraction is reduced.

If alpha divides a fixed x=A/b^m in S (no reduction assumption on A is needed), then x/alpha lies in R. Because N is coprime to b, N divides A. There are only finitely many possible N; for each N, the inequality b^n<=N bounds n. Hence x has finitely many atom divisors. Units are trivial, so this proves IDF.

## 6. Failure of atomicity

Let p be any prime dividing b. It is a nonunit. The element t cannot divide p because p/t=pb/a is not in R. Every other atom has reduced numerator N>1 coprime to b, so it cannot divide p either: membership of p/alpha in R would imply N divides p, contradicting gcd(N,b)=1. Thus p has no atom divisor and cannot have an atomic factorization.

This is an infinite family, not a full classification of either requested set.
