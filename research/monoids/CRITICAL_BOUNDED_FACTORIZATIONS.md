# Critical digit orbits characterize bounded factorizations in cyclic rational semirings

Let a,b be coprime integers with 1<a<b, let t=a/b, put S=N_0[t], R=Z[1/b], P={p:p divides b}, and

    C=(a-1)b/(b-a).

The standard conductor states R intersect [C,infinity) subset S. All multiplicative units of S minus {0} are 1.

## Canonical orbit and the meaning of critical

For x in S minus {0}, start x_0=x. At each step choose the unique c_i in {0,...,a-1} such that x_i-c_i belongs aR and set

    x_(i+1)=(x_i-c_i)/t.

Every state is in S. Once the reduced denominator has been cleared, the states are nonnegative integers, and the recurrence becomes

    T(c)=b floor(c/a).

Call x **critical** if x_i<=C for every i>=0. A state above C never returns below C, since

    (x_i-c_i)/t >= (x_i-(a-1))/t > (C-(a-1))/t=C.

For a general a/b, checking only the first integral terminal is insufficient. For example a=5,b=8 gives C=32/3, but the terminal integer 10 subsequently maps to 16>C. In the special case a=5,b=6, the integer interval [0,24] is invariant under T(c)=6 floor(c/5), so the usual minimal-denominator terminal test <=24 does suffice.

## Theorem: an explicit bound on every nonunit factorization

Let X be the part of the reduced numerator of x supported outside P, and let Omega(X) count its prime factors with multiplicity. If x is critical, every factorization

    x=u_1 ... u_m,  u_i in S minus {0,1},

has

    m <= Omega(X) + floor(log_2 C).

In particular every critical element has a factorization into atoms. For a b-smooth critical element the bound is simply floor(log_2 C).

**Proof.** Choose any nonnegative integral polynomial A_i evaluating to u_i. Let G=product A_i, let D=deg G, and let L_i>=1 be the leading coefficient of A_i. Normalize G from its constant term upward, replacing a X^j by b X^(j+1). All carries are nonnegative. At degree D the resulting terminal is exactly the canonical state x_D, and its coefficient is at least the original leading coefficient of G. Consequently

    product L_i = lc(G) <= x_D <= C.

Thus at most floor(log_2 C) factors have L_i>=2.

Suppose L_i=1 and d=deg A_i. Writing u_i=N/b^d gives

    N=sum_j (A_i)_j a^j b^(d-j).

For every p dividing b, N is congruent to a^d modulo p, and hence gcd(N,b)=1. If d>0, N>=a^d>1. If d=0, L_i=1 would mean u_i=1, which is excluded. Thus every such factor consumes at least one prime, with multiplicity, from the outside-P numerator X. Valuations outside P are nonnegative on S and add in the displayed factorization, so there are at most Omega(X) such factors. This proves the bound.

If x had no atomic factorization, repeatedly split a non-atomic nonunit factor into two nonunits. This would produce arbitrarily long nonunit factorizations, contradicting the bound. QED.

For N_0[5/6], every 2,3-smooth critical element is therefore atomic and every nonunit factorization has length at most 4.

## Converse when at least two denominator primes occur

Assume |P|>=2. Every noncritical x starts an infinite proper divisor chain. Therefore the following conditions on x are equivalent:

1. x is critical;
2. lengths of factorizations of x into arbitrary nonunits are bounded;
3. there is no infinite sequence x=x_0,x_1,... in S with x_i=x_(i+1) h_(i+1), where every h_i is a nonunit.

The theorem proves 1=>2. An infinite proper divisor chain gives factorizations of unbounded length, so 2=>3. We prove the contrapositive of 3=>1.

A noncritical x has an expression

    x=f(t)+t^j y,   f in N_0[X],  y in R,  y>C.

Put B=x/(f(t)+t^j C)>1. Choose B_0 with 1<B_0<B, and also B_0<x if x>1. The case x=1 is critical and does not occur.

The standard dense principal-congruence construction supplies b-smooth h in S, arbitrarily close to 1 from above, with h-1 in a^j R. Explicitly one may use the subgroup generated by p^phi(a^L),q^phi(a^L) for distinct p,q in P and L>=j, choose h between 1+C t^L and 1+(C+1)t^L, and use the conductor on (h-1)/t^L.

Choose a sequence h_i of such nonunits with

    1<h_i<B_0^(2^(-i)),   i>=1.

Put H_n=product_(i=1)^n h_i and x_n=x/H_n. Then 1<H_n<B_0<B, so

    x_n > f(t)+t^j C.

Because H_n is a unit of R and H_n-1 belongs a^j R,

    z_n=(x_n-f(t))/t^j

belongs R. The displayed real inequality gives z_n>C, and the conductor gives z_n in S. Hence x_n=f(t)+t^j z_n belongs S. Moreover x_n=x_(n+1)h_(n+1), with h_(n+1)>1 a nonunit. The x_n are distinct. They are also nonunits: if x<1 then x_n<1, while if x>1 our choice H_n<B_0<x gives x_n>1. This is the required infinite proper divisor chain.

## Scope

This characterizes bounded factorization length and the absence of divisor chains **for individual elements**. It does not prove that noncritical elements lack atomic factorizations: an element can have an atomic factorization and also arbitrarily long nonunit factorizations. For example in N_0[5/6], sufficiently large ordinary integers are noncritical and nevertheless have familiar atomic factorizations in some cases.

It also does not establish IDF of the entire monoid. The bounded-length argument permits infinitely many atom divisors appearing in different bounded-length factorizations. The separate tail analysis addresses actual divisor finiteness for smooth critical elements of N_0[5/6].

## Exact finite threshold for criticality, and a sharper length bound

The full integer orbit can in fact be classified explicitly. Put

    d=b-a,
    K=floor((a-1)/d),
    M=a*(K+1)-1.

If c is the first integral canonical terminal, then

    x is critical  iff  c<=M.                           (4)

To prove this, write k=floor(c/a), so T(c)=bk. If k<=K, then dk<a and

    T(bk)=b floor(bk/a)=b*(k+floor(dk/a))=bk.

Thus the integer orbit reaches a fixed point after at most one step. Moreover c<=M and bk<=bK<=M<=C. Working backwards through canonical digits gives every earlier real state at most C.

If k>K, then dk>=a, so T(bk)=b*(k+floor(dk/a))>=b(k+1). Subsequent integer indices increase strictly and hence escape above C. This proves (4).

There are no nonconstant bounded cycles of the terminal map: all bounded integer orbits enter one of 0,b,2b,...,Kb after at most one step.

For every polynomial representation of a critical x, the canonical terminal at that representation's degree is at most M. Thus the earlier factorization proof strengthens to

    length <= Omega(X)+floor(log_2 M).                  (5)

For a=5,b=6, K=4 and M=24. For a=5,b=8, K=1 and M=9, explaining precisely why the terminal10 is noncritical despite lying below C=32/3. When b>=2a, K=0 and M=a-1, recovering the bounded-coefficient regime.
