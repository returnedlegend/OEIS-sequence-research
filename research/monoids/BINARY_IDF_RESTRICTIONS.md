# Further restrictions on a hypothetical non-IDF family for (2/5,2/3)

Let q=2/5. The exact IDF reduction in PAIR_2_5_2_3.md is the finiteness, for every fixed
positive M coprime to 30, of binary polynomials f with constant and leading
coefficients 1 satisfying

    f(q)=M*3^k/5^n,  n=deg(f), k>=0.

The results below do not establish that finiteness. Novelty is unverified
against the full AoPS discussion and its attached drafts; see SOURCE_STATUS.md.
The known non-atomicity of this pair is credited in the supplied source to
earlier work by BakedPotatoz. Sections 1, 2, 4 and 5 are proved restrictions;
Section 3 labels its sufficient valuation bound as unproved.

## 1. Bounded numbers of runs give only finitely many solutions

Fix M and a bound R on the number of runs of consecutive 1 coefficients
in f. There are only finitely many such f satisfying the displayed equation.

Indeed, write f(X)=sum_(j=0)^n c_j X^j. Then

    (1-X)f(X)=1+sum_(j=1)^n (c_j-c_(j-1))X^j-X^(n+1).

If f has r runs, it begins and ends with 1, so this expression has exactly
2r nonzero terms, each with coefficient +1 or -1 and with distinct
exponents. Evaluation gives a homogeneous S-unit equation

    1+sum_(transitions j) (c_j-c_(j-1))q^j-q^(n+1)
      -M*3^(k+1)/5^(n+1)=0.

Use the finitely generated multiplicative group generated by 2,3,5 and
the prime factors of the fixed M. The equation is nondegenerate: any
nonempty signed sum of the q-powers has a unique term of smallest 2-adic
valuation and cannot vanish. A vanishing proper subsum containing the
last, M-dependent term would leave a nonempty vanishing q-only subsum,
also impossible. The fixed term 1 normalizes the usual homogeneous
scaling ambiguity.

For each r<=R, the standard nondegenerate fixed-length S-unit theorem
therefore gives finitely many tuples. The exponents of the transition
terms determine f, so there are finitely many f.

Thus any infinite family witnessing non-IDF must have unbounded numbers
of 1-runs and 0-runs. This excludes geometric-series and bounded-block
constructions. The argument does not apply an S-unit theorem at variable
length.

This proof follows the corresponding bounded-run observation communicated
by the mixed-pair agent through the root agent during this session.

## 2. Solution degrees have density zero, uniformly in the starting phase

For fixed M, at most one k can work for each n, because binary f(q) lies
in [1,5/3), whose endpoint ratio is less than 3. Put

    theta=log(5)/log(3), eta=log(M)/log(3),
    k_n=ceil(n*theta-eta).

Except at the irrelevant exact-integer endpoint, the logarithm to base 3
of x_n=M*3^(k_n)/5^n is the rotation sequence

    k_n-(n*theta-eta) in [0,1).

The number theta is irrational, because 3 and 5 are multiplicatively
independent. This sequence is therefore uniformly distributed modulo 1.

All finite binary values with constant coefficient 1 lie in the compact
Cantor set

    K = {1+sum_(j>=1) c_j q^j : c_j in {0,1}}.

After fixing m digits, K is covered by 2^m intervals of length at most
(5/3)q^(m+1). Their total length tends to zero, since 2q=4/5<1. Hence K,
and consequently log_3(K), have Lebesgue measure zero.

Uniform distribution now shows that the set of n for which x_n belongs
to K has natural density zero. In particular, solution degrees of the
binary equation have density zero.

More strongly, the conclusion is uniform over phase eta and over starting
indices of intervals of integers: irrational rotations are uniquely
ergodic, and compact measure-zero sets admit finite open covers of
arbitrarily small total length. Equivalently, for every epsilon>0 there
is H_epsilon such that every sufficiently long interval of H consecutive
degrees contains at most epsilon*H solutions, uniformly in M.

This does not rule out an infinite sparse set of degrees.

## 3. A sufficient valuation bound, not a proved theorem

For a binary polynomial of degree n define

    N=sum_(j=0)^n c_j 2^j5^(n-j).

Any uniform bound

    v_3(N) <= c*n+O(1),  c<log(5)/log(3),

would prove IDF: if N=M*3^k and c_0=1, then

    M=N/3^k >= 5^n / 3^(c*n+O(1)),

which tends to infinity with n. The particularly simple conjecture
v_3(N)<=n is supported by finite checks but remains unproved.

Positivity is essential: the signed polynomial

    1-X+X^3+X^4+X^5

evaluates at 2/5 to 3^7/5^5, so the analogous degree bound already fails
for signed digits at degree 5.

## 4. Why bounded-depth congruence sieves cannot suffice by themselves

For a fixed depth J, choose a common multiple T of phi(2^J) and phi(5^J).
There are positive K,L divisible by T with 3^K/5^L arbitrarily close to
1. Indeed the logarithms of 3^T and 5^T have irrational ratio, and their
positive-exponent differences can approximate any bounded real target.
At the same time,

    3^K = 5^L = 1 (mod 2^J),
    3^K = 2^L = 1 (mod 5^J).

Thus candidate ratios can meet arbitrarily long fixed prefix and suffix
congruences while also meeting the real size condition. A uniform proof
needs an argument at depth growing with the exponent sizes, or some
additional global restriction.

## 5. Complete Thue--Morse words and fixed block codings are excluded

Let P_n^+(X) and P_n^-(X) be the binary polynomials whose supports are
respectively the integers j in [0,2^n) with an even or odd number of 1s
in their binary expansion. For n>=2, both polynomials are divisible in
Z[X] by

    S_m(X)=1+X+...+X^(m-1),  m=2^(n-1).

Indeed,

    P_n^+ + P_n^- = S_(2m)(X)=S_m(X)(1+X^m),
    P_n^+ - P_n^- = product_(j=0)^(n-1) (1-X^(2^j)).

The last product contains 1-X^m, hence is also divisible by S_m.
Thus S_m divides both polynomials over Q; because it is monic, the
quotients are in Z[X].

At q=2/5,

    S_m(q)=(5^m-2^m)/(3*5^(m-1)).

Zsigmondy's primitive-prime-divisor theorem supplies a prime p_m dividing
5^m-2^m but no earlier 5^j-2^j. Its exceptions do not occur for the pair
(5,2) and m>=2: the n=2 exception would require 5+2 to be a power of 2,
and the exceptional pair at n=6 is (2,1). Consequently p_m is outside
{2,3,5}, and p_m divides the reduced numerator of P_n^+(q). The same
holds for P_n^-(q) when nonzero. The primitive primes for distinct m
are distinct, since the multiplicative order of 5/2 modulo p_m is m.

Therefore these complete-word families cannot have a fixed outside
numerator part M along an infinite subsequence.

The argument also handles every fixed binary block coding. Let A,B be
fixed binary polynomials of degree less than d, and replace the two
Thue--Morse symbols by their d-letter blocks. The resulting polynomial is

    F_n(X)=A(X)P_n^+(X^d)+B(X)P_n^-(X^d).

It is divisible by S_m(X^d). A primitive prime divisor of
5^(dm)-2^(dm) divides S_m(q^d), because it does not divide
5^d-2^d. Thus it divides the outside numerator part of F_n(q), and new
such primes appear as n grows.

This excludes a natural family with unbounded numbers of runs. It is not
a claim about all automatic words, all morphic words, or arbitrary
truncations of Thue--Morse words.

## 6. Computational status

The exact carry-graph gap algorithm and its audited finite exclusions are
documented separately in BINARY_RATIO_GAP_SEARCH.md. These exclusions are
uniform in M and the individual polynomial degrees, but finite gap bounds
do not prove IDF.

Exact exhaustive computation now proves the valuation bound in Section 3
for every degree through 100. BINARY_VALUATION_SEARCH.md gives the modular
reduction, a table-free exact digit-decoding algorithm, independent
implementations and positive controls, and complete output logs with
checksums. The earlier floating-point MILP reports are superseded by
these exact finite exclusions. The all-degree bound remains unproved.

BINARY_PAIR_ATOMIC_FACTORIZATIONS.md gives an independent proved advance:
terminating membership and complete finite enumeration of atomic
factorizations for every individual element, despite the unresolved IDF
question.

## 7. Any IDF failure already has an atomic witness of length three

Write h=5/3. The following two statements are equivalent:

1. S minus {0} is not IDF.
2. For some finite binary atom alpha_0, the atomic element h^2*alpha_0
   has infinitely many distinct atom divisors in S.

The reverse implication is immediate. For the forward implication, the
exact IDF reduction supplies infinitely many finite binary atoms alpha
with one fixed outside-{3,5} numerator part M. Choose one of them as
alpha_0. Then alpha_0/alpha belongs to R=Z[1/15], and every finite binary
atom lies strictly between 1 and h. Therefore

    (h^2*alpha_0)/alpha > h.

The conductor puts this quotient in S. Thus every atom in the family
divides the single atomic element h^2*alpha_0, which has the displayed
factorization into three atoms.

Moreover, the cofactors of all but finitely many of these atom divisors
have no atomic factorization. To see a precise degree bound, put
n_0=deg(alpha_0). The element x=h^2*alpha_0 has

    h^2 < x < h^3,  v_2(x)=0,  v_5(x)=2-n_0.

An atomic factorization of x contains no q, at most two copies of h,
and otherwise only atoms with negative 5-adic valuation. A finite binary
atom of degree n contributes -n, and another endpoint atom of index i
contributes -i. The valuation equation therefore bounds the sum of all
these degrees and indices by n_0. In particular, any same-M atom alpha
of degree greater than n_0 divides x with a non-atomic cofactor.

This localizes a possible failure of IDF to atomic elements of a simple
form. It neither supplies an infinite atom family nor proves IDF. It also
does not contradict the FFM property of the atom-generated submonoid:
the extra cofactors, if such a family exists, lie outside that submonoid.
