# Two contracting rational generators: a real conductor and a factorization restriction

This is a partial classification, not a solution of both original classification questions.

Let 0<q,r<1 be rational. Choose D to be the least common multiple of their reduced denominators and write q=A/D, r=B/D. Interchange q,r if necessary so A<=B<D. Put

S=N_0[q,r], R=Z[1/D], P={primes dividing D},
g=gcd(A,B), u=A/g, v=B/g, z=g/D.

Then gcd(g,D)=1 and gcd(u,v)=1. The first assertion holds because, for every prime p dividing D, at least one of the original reduced denominators realizes the full p-exponent of D, so its scaled numerator A or B is not divisible by p. Also g equals the gcd of the reduced numerators of q and r: scaling introduces only primes from P, none of which can divide g.

## 1. An elementary uniform real conductor

For i>=0 define the additive numerical monoid

H_i=<u^(i-j) v^j : 0<=j<=i>,

and define integers K_0=0,

K_i=u K_(i-1)+(u-1)v^i  (i>=1).

Every integer N>=K_i belongs to H_i. This follows by induction: choose j in {0,...,u-1} so that N-j*v^i is divisible by u, using gcd(u,v)=1. The quotient is at least K_(i-1). Since H_i contains u*H_(i-1) and v^i, N is in H_i. When u=1 the same statement is immediate and K_i=0.

If u>1 then u<v, and

K_i=(u-1)v*(v^i-u^i)/(v-u)=O(v^i).

Because vz=B/D<1, the constant

C_0 = sum_(i>=0) (K_i+g) z^i
    = g/(1-z) + (u-1)v z/((1-u z)(1-v z))

is finite.

Claim: Every x in R with x>=C_0 belongs to S.

Write x=N/D^n. Recursively choose integers h_i, for 0<=i<n, in the interval [K_i,K_i+g-1] such that

N - sum_(j=0)^i h_j g^j D^(n-j)

is divisible by g^(i+1). This is possible because gcd(g,D)=1 and the interval contains every residue class modulo g. Each h_i belongs to H_i. Let

h_n = (N-sum_(i=0)^(n-1) h_i g^i D^(n-i))/g^n.

It is an integer. Moreover,

h_n z^n = x-sum_(i=0)^(n-1) h_i z^i
         >= sum_(i=n)^infinity (K_i+g)z^i
         >= K_n z^n.

Thus h_n>=K_n, so h_n belongs to H_n. For every i, the element h_i z^i is a nonnegative integer sum of the monomials q^(i-j)r^j. Therefore

x=sum_(i=0)^n h_i z^i

lies in S. This proves the uniform conductor claim.

## 2. Coprime reduced numerators give full positive localization

If g=1, then S=R_(>=0).

Indeed, for any fixed x=N/D^m>0 and any n>=m, write x=(N D^(n-m))/D^n. Since K_n=O(B^n) and B<D, the integer N D^(n-m) is at least K_n for all sufficiently large n. It therefore belongs to H_n. When g=1, the generators of H_n divided by D^n are precisely q^(n-j)r^j. Thus x belongs to S.

Consequently, when g=1, S minus {0} is factorial modulo its units, hence atomic and IDF. Such pairs belong to neither requested answer set.

## 3. Common reduced-numerator factor: units and near-one elements

Suppose g>1. Choose a prime ell dividing g. It is not in P. Every element of S has nonnegative ell-adic valuation. Every element x in S with 0<x<1 must have a polynomial representation with zero constant term. Every remaining monomial has positive ell-adic valuation, so v_ell(x)>0.

Consequently, every positive P-smooth element of S is at least 1. Every multiplicative unit of S is a positive P-smooth rational, as is its inverse. Both are therefore at least 1. Thus the unit group of S minus {0} is {1}.

Assume now that P contains distinct primes p,s. Let m be the part of A supported outside P, so m>1, gcd(m,D)=1, and qR=mR. For each k>=1, the subgroup generated by p^phi(m^k) and s^phi(m^k) is dense in the positive real numbers, and every one of its elements is congruent to 1 modulo m^kR. Density follows from the irrationality of log(p)/log(s).

Choose h_k from this subgroup in the interval

1+C_0 q^k < h_k < 1+(C_0+1)q^k.

Since q^kR=m^kR, the rational y_k=(h_k-1)/q^k belongs to R and exceeds C_0. The conductor implies y_k in S; hence h_k=1+q^k y_k belongs to S. These are P-smooth nonunits and h_k tends to 1 from above.

## 4. Atomicity forces non-IDF when at least two denominator primes occur

Continue with g>1 and |P|>=2. Suppose S minus {0} is atomic. Factor each h_k into atoms. All factors are P-smooth: the valuations at primes outside P are nonnegative and sum to zero. Each factor is therefore at least 1, and every atom is strictly greater than 1. Choosing an atom alpha_k dividing h_k gives

1<alpha_k<=h_k -> 1.

There are infinitely many distinct such atoms. Fix an integer T>2C_0. Eventually alpha_k<2, and

T/alpha_k belongs to R, and T/alpha_k>T/2>C_0.

The conductor puts T/alpha_k in S. Thus infinitely many distinct, and hence nonassociate, atoms divide the same T. This proves

S minus {0} atomic  =>  S minus {0} non-IDF.

Equivalently, IDF implies non-atomic in this regime. Both properties may fail; the implication does not claim a dichotomy.

Also every atom is at most C_0 as a real number: if x>C_0, choose h_k sufficiently near 1 so x/h_k>C_0. Then x=h_k*(x/h_k) is a factorization into nonunits in S.

## 5. One denominator prime excludes both requested behaviors

For completeness suppose |P|=1, say P={p}. The case g=1 is already factorial. In the case g>1 choose ell dividing g. Let w be a fixed nonzero S-element and E=v_ell(w). For every divisor x of w, v_ell(x)<=E. In any nonnegative polynomial representation of x, at least one term has ell-adic valuation at most E. Its total degree is at most E, since both q and r have positive integer ell-adic valuation. Thus, putting t=min(q,r),

x>=t^E.

The cofactor satisfies the same bound, hence t^E<=x<=w/t^E. The part of the reduced numerator of x supported outside p divides the fixed corresponding part of w. There are finitely many such parts, and the remaining power of p lies in a bounded real interval, giving finitely many actual divisors of w. Thus S minus {0} is atomic and IDF.

## Precise scope

For two positive generators less than 1:

- coprime reduced numerators => factorial modulo units;
- at most one denominator prime => atomic and IDF;
- common reduced-numerator factor and at least two denominator primes => atomicity forces non-IDF, and IDF forces non-atomic.

The last bullet does not decide which pairs are atomic, which are IDF, or whether both fail. It is a proved reduction of the remaining classification, not its completion.
