# Rational semiring multiplicative monoids: checked partial results

Problem: classify rational pairs (q,r) for which N_0[q,r] minus {0}, under
multiplication, is (a) atomic and non-IDF, or (b) IDF and non-atomic.

**Status: partial results only. No complete classification is claimed.**

## Stronger verified families for (b)

Write t=a/b in lowest terms, a>=2, and b=product p^(e_p). Assume b has at
least two distinct prime factors and a<p^(e_p) for every prime p dividing b.
Then N_0[t] minus {0} is IDF and non-atomic. Thus every pair (t,r) with
r in N_0[t], and its transpose, belongs to (b).

If in addition a is a prime power, N_0[t,t^(-1)] minus {0} is IDF and
non-atomic. The same applies to (t^i,t^(-j)) for positive coprime i,j.

See [PRIME_POWER_DENOMINATOR_FAMILIES.md](PRIME_POWER_DENOMINATOR_FAMILIES.md)
for self-contained proofs, including the nontrivial unit group in the
reciprocal case. Examples include (5/144,0) and (4/225,225/4). An explicit
element with no atom divisor in both N_0[5/144] and its reciprocal semiring is

    243/128 = 1 + 4*(5/144) + 630*(5/144)^2.

The reciprocal pair (5/144,144/5) is therefore another explicit example.
See RECIPROCAL_ALGORITHM.md and reciprocal_factorizations.py for an exact
finite atom-divisor and factorization algorithm in the reciprocal family.

These hypotheses compare a to maximal prime-power factors, not just the
individual primes. The older condition a<min{p:p|b}, proved in
IDF_NONATOMIC_FAMILY.md, is a special case.

For every fixed coprime a,b with a>1 and at least two primes dividing b,
N_0[a/b^N] is therefore IDF and non-atomic for all sufficiently large N.

No novelty claim is made: the user supplied detailed October–November
progress posts, but live discussion access and the full attached drafts
remain unavailable. Several of our reductions recover credited results.
See [SOURCE_STATUS.md](SOURCE_STATUS.md).

## A complete mixed family belonging to neither (a) nor (b)

Let ell,p be distinct primes, q=ell^s/p^k<1, and r>1 whose reduced
denominator is supported on {ell,p}. Then N_0[q,r] minus {0} is atomic and
IDF, with finitely many divisor classes for each element. Depending on the
relative sizes, it either has finitely many actual divisors, becomes a
positive ring localization, or has a cyclic nontrivial unit group.

See [MIXED_TWO_PRIME_FAMILY.md](MIXED_TWO_PRIME_FAMILY.md) for the complete
proof and a broader sufficient criterion that permits composite numerators.

- [PAIR_3_10_7_5.md](PAIR_3_10_7_5.md) recovers ppdppd's known result:
  the only positive integers admitting atomic factorizations are powers

  no solution of that remaining equation is claimed.

## A broader one-generator reduction

Let t=a/b be reduced with 0<t<1, a>1, and at least two distinct prime divisors
of b. Put S=N_0[t], R=Z[1/b], P={primes dividing b}, and
C=(a-1)b/(b-a).

Then S minus {0} cannot be both atomic and IDF. More precisely, if it is
atomic, it is non-IDF; equivalently, if it is IDF, it is non-atomic.

Proof:

1. Every x in R with x>=C lies in S. This is the real-conductor argument in
   IDF_NONATOMIC_FAMILY.md and does not require a to be smaller than the
   primes dividing b. Alternatively, for x=N/b^n choose c in {0,...,a-1}
   with N-c*b^n divisible by a. Then y=(x-c)/t has denominator dividing
   b^(n-1) and y>=(C-(a-1))/t=C. Induction on n proves the assertion.
2. Fix a prime ell dividing a. Every S-element has nonnegative ell-adic
   valuation. Every S-element less than 1 has zero constant coefficient in
   each polynomial representation, so its ell-adic valuation is positive.
   Hence every P-smooth S-element is at least 1. In particular the only
   multiplicative unit is 1.
3. Choose distinct primes p,s in P. For k>=1, set L_k=phi(a^k). The subgroup
   generated by p^L_k and s^L_k is dense in the positive reals and all its
   elements are congruent to 1 modulo a^k in R. Choose h_k from this group
   with 1+C*t^k < h_k < 1+(C+1)*t^k. Then
   z_k=(h_k-1)/t^k belongs to R and is greater than C. Thus z_k lies in S
   and h_k=1+t^k*z_k lies in S. The P-smooth nonunits h_k tend to 1 from above.
4. Suppose S minus {0} is atomic. Factor h_k into atoms. Every factor is
   P-smooth: outside-P valuations are nonnegative and sum to zero. Each
   factor is therefore at least 1, and each atom is strictly greater than
   1. Choosing one atom alpha_k gives 1<alpha_k<=h_k. There must be infinitely
   many distinct such atoms, since h_k tends to 1.
5. Choose an integer B>2C. For all sufficiently large k, alpha_k<2. Since
   alpha_k is P-smooth, B/alpha_k belongs to R, and B/alpha_k>B/2>C. The
   conductor puts B/alpha_k in S. Thus infinitely many distinct atoms divide
   this one fixed B. Units are trivial, so they are nonassociate. This is
   non-IDF, as claimed.

This does not decide whether a given t outside the explicit family is atomic
or IDF, nor does it classify arbitrary nonredundant two-generator pairs.

## Audit of the supplied discussion

Source supplied by user:
https://artofproblemsolving.com/polymath/mitprimes2026/f
The live page was inaccessible through this environment's network proxy; the
analysis used the three pasted files supplied by the user.

Reliable special-case reductions in those excerpts include negative generators
(ring localizations), generators each zero or at least one (local finiteness),
and the denominator-prime antichain lemma (at most one denominator prime
implies IDF). The one-generator prime-power denominator case is atomic and IDF.

The excerpts do not establish their repeated claims that every pair is atomic
and IDF. In particular:

- At a denominator prime, v_p(d)<=v_p(x) does not follow from d dividing x in
  the monoid: 3 divides 2 in N_0[2/3] because 2=3*(2/3).
- Equal outside-denominator valuations do not imply that two elements are
  associates in the submonoid.
- Rational polynomial evaluation is not injective, so coefficient comparison
  and replacing a generator by its reciprocal need separate justification.
- A fixed-length S-unit theorem cannot be applied to arbitrarily long sums
  without proving the necessary bound.

The explicit (b) family above disproves the conclusion that both requested
sets are empty. Complete classification remains unproved in these notes.

## Further proved reductions

- TWO_SMALL_GENERATORS.md proves a uniform real conductor for every pair
  0<q,r<1. Coprime reduced numerators give full positive localization. With a
  common numerator factor and at least two denominator primes, atomicity
  forces non-IDF. This covers genuine, nonredundant two-generator semirings.
- STRONGER_CYCLIC_REDUCTIONS.md proves non-atomicity whenever a denominator
  maximal prime-power factor exceeds the numerator a. It also proves the unique-polynomial
  representation lemma and, for b>=2a, an exact formal description of atoms.
- check_conductor.py validates the constructive conductor algorithm with exact
  rational arithmetic on 300 targets across five pairs. This finite check is
  not a proof of classification; the general theorem is proved in the notes.

- LIMITS_OF_CONDUCTOR_ARGUMENTS.md constructs abstract atomic monoids with a
  real conductor and atoms tending to 1. This rules out one proposed general
  argument, but makes no assertion of additive closure or finite semiring
  generation. It is not an example for part (a).

No complete classification has been obtained. In particular, these arguments
still do not decide atomicity or IDF for the cyclic case t=5/6. This is a
limitation of the present work, not a claim about the current literature.
