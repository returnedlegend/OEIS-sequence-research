# Finite carry certificates extend the nearby ternary IDF family

This extends NEARBY_TERNARY_IDF_FAMILY.md into its previously omitted
congruence class. It does not settle the original pair(3/10,7/5), whose
parameter n=2 is excluded. Novelty relative to the full discussion is
unverified.

## 1. Setup and facts not using the congruence exclusion

Let

    n>2, gcd(n,21)=1, |{p:p|5n}|>=2,
    q=3/(5n), r=7/5, S=N_0[q,r], P={p:p|5n}.

The digit identities, conductor, units, and perturbation arguments in
sections1–5 of the nearby-family proof do not use n!=1 modulo5. They give

    S=qS disjoint-union(1+qS) disjoint-union(r+qS),
    D={0,1,r}, C=r/(1-q)<2, U(S minus{0})={1}.

Every atom has a finite D-digit expression; every atom other than q is at
least1. All nonzero critical elements are finite D-digit expressions, and
criticality is inherited by divisors via the prefix/digit-limit inequality.
At every prime p|n with p!=5, the highest digit of a nonzero finite D-word
of degree N has unique least p-adic valuation, giving

    v_p(x)=-N*v_p(n)<=0.                                  (1)

These statements remain valid when n=1 modulo5.

## 2. Negative5-adic valuation cannot recover under a prefix

If v_5(x)<0, then v_5(qx)<=-2. Each digit d in D has valuation at least-1
(with the usual convention at d=0). Thus

    v_5(d+qx)=v_5(qx)<0.                                  (2)

Consequently every suffix of a5-integral finite D-word is itself5-integral.
The highest nonzero digit cannot be r, whose valuation is-1, so such a word
starts, in reverse construction, from the terminal digit1.

Let F_n be the least set containing1 and closed under all maps

    x -> d+qx, d in D,

whenever the image is5-integral. Then F_n is EXACTLY the set of positive
critical elements with nonnegative5-adic valuation. This is an all-height
statement, not an inference from a truncated search.

A finite set F containing1, consisting of positive5-integral values, and
closed under every5-integral image of these maps certifies F_n subset F.
When generated by the reachable-state search, F consists of critical values
and equals F_n. The implementation generates reachable states and then
independently verifies closure. If a search cutoff is reached, it returns
'inconclusive'; no termination claim is inferred from a cutoff.

## 3. Finite closure proves IDF

Suppose F_n is finite. Every atom in F_n belongs to a finite exception set.
Every other atom has negative5-adic valuation, and (1) gives nonpositive
valuation at all other denominator primes. Its reduced numerator therefore
has no prime factor in P.

For a fixed x in S let X be its reduced numerator with the P-prime factors
removed. If alpha is an atom divisor outside F_n and alpha!=q, write
alpha=A/B in lowest terms. Then

    A|X, B is P-smooth, alpha>=1, hence B<=A.

This is a finite set. Adding q and the finite exceptions F_n proves IDF.

No positive integer greater than1 admits an atomic factorization. Indeed
choose p|n,p!=5. By (1), an atomic product with nonnegative p-adic valuation
can contain only degree-zero D-word atoms. The only such nonunit candidate
is r, and every positive power of r has negative5-adic valuation. Thus the
product cannot be an integer. Non-atomicity follows, regardless of whether
P-smooth atoms exist.

Here q=3/230, r=7/5. The exact5-integral reachable set is

All entries are rational equalities and exact divisibility checks. Closure

Therefore N_0[3/230,7/5] minus{0} is IDF and non-atomic.

The only P-smooth state in F_46 is1, where P={2,5,23}: the other numerators
have outside factors13,431, or19*29*179. Outside F_46, a P-smooth atom would
have numerator1 and value at most1, impossible except for the separately

The atom q+r=65/46 has positive5-adic valuation; this example explains why
a finite exception argument is needed instead of a blanket valuation sign.

## 5. A contrasting certified example with a smooth atom

At n=446,

    q=3/2230, r=7/5, q+r=625/446.

The closure has25 states, of which only1 and625/446 are P-smooth, with
P={2,5,223}. The polynomial X+Y is the unique nonnegative polynomial
representing q+r: its q-degree-zero part is forced to r by residue modulo3,
and the remainder divided by q is1, with its unique constant expression.
Thus q+r is an atom. It divides the integer2, because

    2/(q+r)=892/625 > C=3122/2227,

and this cofactor is in R, so the conductor puts it in S. The monoid is
nevertheless IDF and non-atomic by the finite closure certificate, and no
integer greater than1 has an atomic factorization. This demonstrates that
the certificate method covers examples with P-smooth atoms as well as
examples having no such atoms.

## 6. Exact finite-range certification

The attached JSON file stores a closed reachable set for every integer n
satisfying

    4<=n<=10000, n=1 modulo5, gcd(n,21)=1.

There are1142 such parameters. Every stored graph was independently checked
for containing1,5-integrality, and closure under all allowed prefix maps.
The largest graph has30 states, at n=4196; no search hit its cutoff.

Combined with the analytical theorem for n!=1 modulo5, this proves IDF and
non-atomicity for EVERY parameter in the finite range

    2<n<=10000, gcd(n,21)=1, |P(5n)|>=2.

This finite-range statement is rigorous because each parameter has a complete
closed graph certificate. It is not a proof that the graph search terminates
for every integer n. General termination, and a uniform all-n classification
of this parameter family, remain unproved here.

## 7. Complete factorization decisions from a closed graph

Let M=max{v_5(z):z in F_n}, replacing M by0 if necessary. A critical divisor
y of a critical x has nonpositive valuations at every p|n,p!=5, and its
positive5-adic valuation is at most M. If X is the outside-P numerator of x,
its reduced numerator therefore divides5^M*X. Also

    y>=q^(v_3(y))>=q^(v_3(x)).

Thus all divisors of a critical x lie in the finite list with numerator
dividing5^M*X and P-smooth denominator at most numerator/q^(v_3(x)). Exact
membership tests decide which candidates are divisors, giving an atom oracle.
Noncritical elements are already reducible by the perturbation argument.

For arbitrary x, combine the finite exceptional set F_n with q and the
nonexceptional numerator/denominator list in section3, then apply the oracle
and cofactor membership. This gives all atom divisors. Finally copies of q
are bounded by v_3(x), and every other atom exceeds1; a finite real-product
bound gives complete enumeration of all atomic factorizations. The method
does not use a negative5-adic length bound, which would fail in this family.
