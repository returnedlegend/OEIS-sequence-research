# A specific genuinely two-generator non-atomic monoid

This is an independent reconstruction of a known result from the supplied
discussion: ppdppd's October 24 post already establishes non-atomicity for
this pair and the classification of integers admitting atomic
factorizations. The supplied November 9 discussion still leaves IDF open.
No novelty is claimed for the results in this note.

Let q=3/10, r=7/5, and S=N_0[q,r]. Then the multiplicative monoid
S\{0} is non-atomic. More precisely, every multiplicative atom alpha has
v_5(alpha)<=0, so the element 5 has no factorization into atoms. A positive
integer admits an atomic factorization exactly when it is a power of 2,
including the empty factorization of 1.

The argument does not decide IDF. In particular it does not prove that 5
has no atom divisor: the assertion is only that 5 has no atomic
factorization. The monoid has trivial units.

## 1. Units and a real conductor

Put R=Z[1/10]. The only nonnegative polynomial expression in q,r for 1
is the constant 1. Any monomial involving r alone, with positive exponent,
already exceeds 1. A positive constant term forces it to be 1 and leaves
no other terms. If there is no term of q-degree 0, every term has positive
3-adic valuation, so the sum cannot be 1. Multiplying polynomial
expressions for two mutually inverse S-elements therefore forces both
to be the constant 1. Thus S\{0} has unit group {1}.

Every x in R with x>=2 belongs S. Write x=N/10^n and induct on n.
The case n=0 is immediate. For n>=1 choose d from {0,1,r} with the same
residue as x in R/3R; these three digits have residues 0,1,2 respectively.
Then

  y=(x-d)/q

has denominator dividing 10^{n-1}. Indeed the numerator of x-d over 10^n
is divisible by 3, and division by q=3/10 removes one factor 10 from
the denominator. Because d<=r and 2=r+2q,

  y >= (2-r)/q = 2.

Induction gives y in S, so x=d+q*y belongs S.

## 2. Smooth elements approaching 1

For each L>=1 let E_L=phi(3^L). The subgroup generated by 2^{E_L} and
5^{E_L} is dense in the positive reals, because log(2)/log(5) is irrational.
Its elements are supported on the primes 2,5 and congruent to 1 modulo
3^L R. Choose h_L from it with

  1+2*q^L < h_L < 1+3*q^L.

Then (h_L-1)/q^L belongs R and exceeds 2, so the conductor puts this
quotient in S. Therefore h_L belongs S. The h_L tend to 1 from above
and have an infinite pairwise distinct subsequence, which we use below.

## 3. A block larger than 2 forces reducibility

Suppose x=f+q^i*c, where f belongs S, i>=0, c belongs R, and c>2.
For L>=i put

  z_L=(x/h_L-f)/q^i
     =c+x*(h_L^{-1}-1)/q^i.

The congruence h_L-1 in 3^L R, together with the fact that h_L is a unit
of R, gives z_L in R. Also z_L tends to c>2. Hence z_L belongs S for
sufficiently large L, and

  x=h_L*(f+q^i*z_L)

is a factorization in S. The first factor exceeds 1. The second can equal
the only unit 1 only when h_L=x, which occurs at most once along our
distinct subsequence. Thus x is reducible.

This lemma applies to an entire coefficient in R at a given q-degree;
it does not require that coefficient to be an integer. It also shows
that every atom is at most 2, by taking i=0, f=0, c=x.

## 4. Every atom has a short digit description

Take any nonnegative polynomial expression for an atom alpha. A monomial
q^i*r^j with j>=2 is impossible. Indeed

  r^2=1+2q+4q^2

would replace that monomial by nonnegative terms and expose a block

  q^{i+2}*(4*r^{j-2}),

whose parenthesized coefficient exceeds 2. Section 3 would make alpha
reducible. Consequently the expression has r-degree at most 1.

Group it by q-degree, writing each coefficient as A_i+B_i*r with
A_i,B_i nonnegative integers. Any coefficient greater than 2 again makes
alpha reducible. Since 1+r>2 and 2r>2, the only possible coefficients are

  0, 1, 2, r.

If a coefficient 2 occurs at degree i with any nonzero coefficient at a
higher degree, use

  2q^i=rq^i+2q^{i+1}.

Carry the 2 through zero coefficients until the first higher nonzero
coefficient d is reached. The resulting coefficient 2+d exceeds 2,
contradicting section 3. Thus 2 can occur only at the highest nonzero
q-degree.

Finally, whenever a terminal 2 at degree n is immediately preceded by r,
collapse

  rq^{n-1}+2q^n=2q^{n-1}.

After finitely many collapses, every atom has an expression

  alpha=sum_{i=0}^n d_i*q^i,

where the highest coefficient d_n is nonzero, every lower coefficient
belongs {0,1,r}, and one of the following holds:

* d_n=1;
* d_n=r;
* d_n=2, with n=0 or d_{n-1} in {0,1}.

This argument starts from a finite polynomial expression and uses finitely
many substitutions; no infinite digit expansion or unproved membership
algorithm is needed. Leading zero coefficients are allowed, and trailing
zeros have been removed. The endpoint n=0 is included.

## 5. The valuation at 5 of every atom is nonpositive

If n=0, the displayed nonzero coefficient is 1,2, or r. Their 5-adic
valuations are 0,0,-1, respectively; 1 is of course a unit rather than
an atom.

Suppose n>=1. There are three cases.

If d_n=r, the term rq^n has valuation -(n+1). All terms of lower
q-degree have valuation at least -n, so the minimum is unique and

  v_5(alpha)=-(n+1).

If d_n=1, the term q^n has valuation -n. The only lower-degree term that
can have this same valuation is rq^{n-1}. If it is absent the minimum
is unique. If it is present, their sum is

  q^n+rq^{n-1}=17*3^{n-1}/10^n,

which still has valuation -n; all remaining terms have valuation at
least -(n-1). Thus v_5(alpha)=-n in either event.

If d_n=2, the normalization in section 4 ensures d_{n-1} is not r.
The term 2q^n then has valuation -n, while all lower terms have valuation
at least -(n-1). Hence again v_5(alpha)=-n.

Therefore every atom alpha has v_5(alpha)<=0. Any finite product of
atoms has nonpositive 5-adic valuation, whereas v_5(5)=1. The element 5
is a nonunit of S with no atomic factorization. This proves non-atomicity.

## 6. The atom 2 and the integer atomic factorizations

Every nonnegative polynomial expression for 2 is one of

  F_n(X,Y)=2X^n+Y*(1+X+...+X^{n-1}),   n>=0,

where the Y-part is empty for n=0. To prove this, group such an expression
by its X-degree. Its degree-zero part is a nonnegative polynomial in r
with value at most 2, hence is one of 0,1,2,r,r^2. Modulo 3R, the required
residue is 2, so only 2 and r are possible. If it is 2, all positive-degree
terms vanish. If it is r, the remaining expression is q times another
polynomial representing (2-r)/q=2. Repeating this argument terminates
because the original polynomial has finite X-degree, yielding F_n.

Each F_n is multiplicatively irreducible in N_0[X,Y]. This is immediate
for F_0=2. For n>=1, its Y-degree is 1, so in any factorization one factor
G depends on X alone. It divides both 2X^n and 1+X+...+X^{n-1}. A
nonnegative polynomial dividing the monomial 2X^n is itself a monomial;
the constant coefficient 1 of the second polynomial then forces G=1.
Consequently every factorization of 2 in S has a unit factor, proving
that 2 is an atom.

The normal forms and valuations in sections 4 and 5 show that 2 is the
only atom with 5-adic valuation zero: every positive-degree form and the
constant form r have negative valuation, while the constant form 1 is
a unit. In any atomic factorization of a positive integer, all atom
valuations at 5 are nonpositive and their sum must be nonnegative.
They must therefore all be zero, so every atom in the factorization is
2. Conversely each power 2^k has the evident factorization into k copies
of the atom 2. This proves the claimed integer classification.

## Scope

The proof recovers non-atomicity for the genuine mixed pair
(3/10,7/5), beyond failure of ACCP. It does not determine whether this
monoid is IDF or non-IDF, and it does not require an answer to that
question.
